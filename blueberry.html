Blueberries

  1. Blueberries are one of the few naturally blue foods.

    Their deep blue color comes from powerful plant pigments called anthocyanins, which are also antioxidants.

  2. They're considered a "superfood."

    Blueberries are rich in vitamin C, vitamin K, manganese, fiber, and antioxidants while being low in calories.

  3. Blueberries are native to North America.

    Indigenous peoples harvested wild blueberries for thousands of years before they were commercially cultivated.

  4. They have a natural protective coating.

    The whitish, dusty layer on fresh blueberries is called bloom. It's a natural wax that helps protect the fruit and keep it fresh.

  5. Blueberries float in water.

    Like apples, they contain tiny air pockets that make them less dense than water.

  6. There are two main types.

    Highbush blueberries are the large ones commonly found in supermarkets. Lowbush (wild) blueberries are smaller but often have a more intense flavor.

  7. The inside is colorful too.

    Unlike many fruits, blueberries are blue or purple not just on the outside but also throughout much of the flesh.

  8. They can improve in sweetness after a sunny season.

    Warm, sunny weather helps the berries produce more natural sugars, resulting in sweeter fruit.

  9. A blueberry bush can live for decades.

    With proper care, healthy bushes can continue producing fruit for 30–50 years or even longer.

  10. Blueberries are popular with wildlife.

    Birds, bears, deer, foxes, and many other animals love blueberries, making them an important food source in many ecosystems.

Bonus fact: A cup of blueberries contains hundreds of individual berries—typically around 65–100 blueberries, depending on their size!
