Dragon Fruit

  1. Dragon fruit grows on a cactus.

    Unlike most fruits, dragon fruit grows on a climbing cactus, making it well adapted to warm, dry climates.

  2. It blooms only at night.

    Dragon fruit plants produce enormous, fragrant flowers that usually open after sunset and wilt by the next morning. They're often called "Queen of the Night" flowers.

  3. There are several types of dragon fruit.

    The most common varieties have white flesh with pink skin, red flesh with pink skin, or white flesh with yellow skin (the sweetest).

  4. The tiny black seeds are edible.

    They add a pleasant crunch and are rich in healthy fats, much like kiwi seeds.

  5. It's low in calories but high in nutrients.

    Dragon fruit is a good source of vitamin C, fiber, magnesium, and antioxidants while being naturally low in calories.

  6. Its bright color comes from natural pigments.

    Red-fleshed dragon fruit contains betalains, powerful antioxidants that also give beets their deep red color.

  7. It can produce fruit several times a year.

    In warm climates, a mature dragon fruit cactus may flower and fruit multiple times annually.

  8. It originally comes from the Americas.

    Although it's strongly associated with Southeast Asia today, dragon fruit is native to southern Mexico and Central America.

  9. The flowers are pollinated by night animals.

    Bats and moths are among the main natural pollinators because the flowers open after dark.

  10. It's known by many names.

    Depending on the country, dragon fruit is also called pitaya, pitahaya, or strawberry pear.

Bonus fact: Despite its dramatic appearance—with bright pink skin and green "scales"—dragon fruit has a mild, subtly sweet flavor. Many people describe it as a mix between kiwi and pear, with a texture similar to kiwi because of its tiny edible seeds.
