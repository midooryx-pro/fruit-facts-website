Strawberries

  1. They're not actually berries.

    Despite the name, strawberries aren't true botanical berries. Bananas, grapes, and even tomatoes are true berries, but strawberries are classified as aggregate accessory fruits.

  2. The "seeds" are actually tiny fruits.

    Those little yellow dots on the outside are called achenes. Each achene contains a single seed, making every strawberry a collection of around 200 tiny fruits.

  3. They belong to the rose family.

    Strawberries are members of the Rosaceae family, making them relatives of roses, apples, pears, cherries, peaches, and almonds.

  4. They're packed with vitamin C.

    Just 8 medium strawberries provide more vitamin C than one orange, covering around 100% of an adult's daily requirement.

  5. They can help your heart.

    Strawberries are rich in antioxidants such as anthocyanins, which give them their red color and may help reduce inflammation, lower blood pressure, and support heart health.

  6. The garden strawberry is a happy accident.

    The strawberries we eat today are a hybrid created in the 18th century by crossing wild North American and Chilean strawberry species.

  7. There are hundreds of varieties.

    Farmers grow more than 600 varieties of strawberries around the world, each with different flavors, sizes, colors, and growing conditions.

  8. They don't keep ripening after they're picked.

    Unlike bananas or tomatoes, strawberries stop ripening once harvested. That's why they're picked only when fully ripe.

  9. The world's largest strawberry was enormous.

    World's largest strawberry weighed 289 grams (10.19 ounces) — about the size of a small melon.

  10. Almost every part of the plant is useful.

    Besides the fruit, strawberry leaves are often used to make herbal tea, the flowers attract pollinators, and the plants spread naturally by sending out runners that grow into new strawberry plants.

Bonus fact: Strawberries are about 91% water, making them a refreshing, low-calorie snack — roughly 32 calories per 100 grams.
