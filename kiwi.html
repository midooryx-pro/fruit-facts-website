Kiwi

  1. Kiwifruit originally came from China.

    It was once known as the Chinese gooseberry before being renamed "kiwifruit" in the 1950s after New Zealand's famous kiwi bird.

  2. It's packed with vitamin C.

    One medium kiwifruit provides more vitamin C than an orange, helping support the immune system and healthy skin.

  3. The skin is edible.

    Although fuzzy, the skin is safe to eat and contains extra fiber and antioxidants. Many people simply wash it well and eat it whole.

  4. There are green and golden varieties.

    Green kiwis have a tangy flavor, while golden kiwis are sweeter, smoother-skinned, and less acidic.

  5. Kiwis grow on vines, not trees.

    They grow on large, woody climbing vines that need strong support structures, much like grapevines.

  6. They contain a unique enzyme.

    Kiwifruit contains actinidin, an enzyme that helps break down proteins. That's why fresh kiwi can tenderize meat and sometimes prevents gelatin desserts from setting.

  7. They have more potassium than many people realize.

    Kiwis are a good source of potassium, which helps muscles and nerves function properly and supports healthy blood pressure.

  8. The tiny black seeds are edible.

    All of the small black seeds inside a kiwi are perfectly safe to eat and add a slight crunch.

  9. Kiwifruit can continue ripening after picking.

    Like bananas and avocados, kiwis ripen after harvest. Placing them in a paper bag with an apple or banana speeds up the process because of the ethylene gas those fruits release.

  10. New Zealand made kiwi famous—but doesn't grow the most.

    Although New Zealand is closely associated with kiwifruit, China is the world's largest producer, followed by countries such as New Zealand and Italy.

Bonus fact: The bright green color of most kiwifruit comes from chlorophyll, the same natural pigment that makes leaves green. Unlike many fruits, ripe green kiwis retain much of this chlorophyll instead of developing yellow or orange pigments.
