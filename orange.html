Oranges

  1. Oranges are the world's most popular citrus fruit.

    Millions of tons are grown every year, making them one of the most widely cultivated fruits on Earth.

  2. They're a fantastic source of vitamin C.

    One medium orange provides most or all of an adult's daily vitamin C needs, helping support the immune system, skin, and wound healing.

  3. Oranges are a hybrid fruit.

    Sweet oranges are a natural hybrid of pomelos and mandarins, created thousands of years ago through natural crossbreeding.

  4. There are hundreds of orange varieties.

    Popular types include Navel, Valencia, Blood, and Cara Cara oranges, each with its own flavor and characteristics.

  5. The peel is highly aromatic.

    Orange peels contain tiny oil glands filled with fragrant essential oils, which are used in perfumes, cleaners, and flavorings.

  6. The white layer under the peel is nutritious.

    The spongy white part, called the pith, is rich in fiber and beneficial plant compounds, even though many people remove it.

  7. Blood oranges get their color naturally.

    Their deep red flesh comes from anthocyanins, the same antioxidants that give blueberries and red cabbage their color.

  8. Orange trees can live for decades.

    With proper care, they can continue producing fruit for 50 years or more, and some live well over a century.

  9. The flowers smell amazing.

    Orange blossoms are famous for their sweet fragrance and are often used to make perfumes, teas, and orange blossom water.

  10. Brazil grows the most oranges.

    Brazil is the world's largest producer of oranges and is also the leading producer of orange juice.

Bonus fact: The word "orange" was originally the name of the fruit. The color was later named after it—in English, there wasn't a specific word for the color orange before the fruit became widely known!
