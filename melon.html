Watermelon

  1. Watermelon is about 92% water.

    That's why it's one of the most refreshing fruits, especially on hot summer days.

  2. It's both a fruit and a vegetable.

    Botanically, watermelon is a fruit because it develops from a flower. Agriculturally, it's often considered a vegetable because it's grown similarly to crops like pumpkins and cucumbers.

  3. Watermelons belong to the cucumber family.

    They are members of the Cucurbitaceae family, making them relatives of cucumbers, pumpkins, squash, and cantaloupes.

  4. There are more than 1,200 varieties.

    Watermelons come in many shapes, sizes, and colors—including red, pink, orange, yellow, and even white flesh.

  5. The rind is edible.

    While many people throw it away, the white rind can be pickled, stir-fried, or candied and is rich in nutrients.

  6. Seedless watermelons aren't genetically modified.

    They are created by crossing watermelon plants with different chromosome numbers, producing fruits with soft, undeveloped seeds.

  7. Watermelon contains lycopene.

    The red flesh gets its color from lycopene, a powerful antioxidant that's also found in tomatoes.

  8. The world's heaviest watermelon weighed over 159 kg (350 lb).

    Giant watermelons can grow to incredible sizes under the right conditions.

  9. Every part of the fruit is edible.

    The flesh, seeds, and rind can all be eaten. In many countries, roasted watermelon seeds are a popular snack.

  10. Ancient Egyptians loved watermelons.

    Watermelons were cultivated thousands of years ago and were even placed in some tombs, including those of pharaohs, to provide food for the afterlife.

Bonus fact: Square watermelons really exist! They are grown inside transparent square boxes while they're still developing. The fruit simply grows into the shape of the container, making it easier to stack and transport—though they're often sold as novelty items rather than for eating.
