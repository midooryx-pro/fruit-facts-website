Pineapple

  1. A pineapple is made up of many fruits.

    Each pineapple is actually a multiple fruit, formed when dozens (or even hundreds) of individual flowers fuse into one large fruit.

  2. They don't grow on trees.

    Pineapples grow on a short, ground-level tropical plant with long, spiky leaves.

  3. It takes a long time to grow.

    A single pineapple usually takes 18–24 months to grow from planting to harvest.

  4. One plant usually produces one pineapple at a time.

    After harvesting its main fruit, the plant may produce smaller fruits called ratoons before it is replaced.

  5. Pineapples contain bromelain.

    Bromelain is a natural enzyme that breaks down proteins, which is why fresh pineapple can tenderize meat and sometimes makes your mouth feel slightly sore after eating a lot of it.

  6. The top can grow into a new plant.

    The leafy crown of a pineapple can be planted to grow another pineapple plant, although it takes patience before it produces fruit.

  7. They are rich in vitamin C and manganese.

    Pineapples are an excellent source of vitamin C and provide plenty of manganese, which helps support bone health and metabolism.

  8. They were once a symbol of wealth.

    In Europe during the 17th and 18th centuries, pineapples were so rare and expensive that they became a symbol of luxury and hospitality.

  9. The world's largest producer is Costa Rica.

    Costa Rica is one of the leading producers and the largest exporter of fresh pineapples, shipping them worldwide.

  10. Pineapples don't continue to sweeten after harvest.

    Unlike bananas or mangoes, pineapples become softer after picking but do not get sweeter, so they should be harvested when ripe.

Bonus fact: The name "pineapple" comes from early European explorers, who thought the fruit looked like a pine cone (called a "pine") combined with an apple. In many other languages, it's called ananas, a word that comes from an Indigenous South American language and means "excellent fruit."
