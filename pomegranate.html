Pomegranate

  1. Pomegranates are one of the oldest cultivated fruits.

    They have been grown for over 5,000 years and were prized by many ancient civilizations.

  2. A single fruit can contain hundreds of seeds.

    Most pomegranates have between 200 and 1,400 juicy seeds, called arils.

  3. The edible part is the aril.

    Each seed is surrounded by a juicy, ruby-red covering that's sweet and tart. Most people eat the entire aril, including the seed inside.

  4. They're packed with antioxidants.

    Pomegranates are rich in powerful antioxidants called punicalagins and anthocyanins, which help protect cells from damage.

  5. The flowers are beautiful and edible.

    Bright red-orange pomegranate flowers attract bees and other pollinators, and in some cultures they're used to make tea.

  6. Pomegranate trees are long-lived.

    With proper care, they can live for 100 years or more and continue producing fruit for many decades.

  7. They naturally resist dry conditions.

    Pomegranate trees are drought-tolerant and grow well in hot, sunny climates with relatively little water.

  8. The fruit doesn't ripen much after picking.

    Unlike bananas or mangoes, pomegranates should be harvested when fully ripe because they don't become sweeter after they're picked.

  9. The peel has many traditional uses.

    Although it's not usually eaten, the thick rind has been used historically to make natural dyes, leather tanning agents, and herbal preparations.

  10. Pomegranates are symbols of prosperity and fertility.

    Throughout history, they've appeared in the art, mythology, and traditions of many cultures, representing abundance, life, and good fortune.

Bonus fact: The deep red juice of a pomegranate can stain fabrics and hands very easily because it's rich in natural pigments called anthocyanins—the same compounds that give blueberries and red cabbage their vibrant colors.
