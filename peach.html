Peaches

  1. Peaches originated in China.

    They were first cultivated there over 4,000 years ago and have long been a symbol of longevity, prosperity, and good luck.

  2. Peaches are members of the rose family.

    They belong to the Rosaceae family, making them relatives of apples, pears, cherries, plums, almonds, and roses.

  3. The fuzzy skin has a purpose.

    The soft fuzz helps protect the fruit from insects, moisture loss, and certain diseases as it grows.

  4. Nectarines are just fuzz-free peaches.

    Nectarines are not a different species—they're peaches with a naturally occurring genetic mutation that gives them smooth skin.

  5. The pit contains a seed.

    Inside the hard stone (pit) is a seed that can grow into a new peach tree, though it won't necessarily produce the same variety of peach.

  6. Peaches are packed with nutrients.

    They're a good source of vitamins A, C, and E, along with fiber and antioxidants that support overall health.

  7. Peach trees can produce fruit for many years.

    A healthy tree typically bears fruit for 10–20 years, producing hundreds of peaches each season.

  8. There are two main flesh types.

    Freestone peaches have a pit that separates easily from the flesh, making them ideal for eating fresh. Clingstone peaches have flesh that clings tightly to the pit and are often used for canning.

  9. Peaches continue to ripen after picking.

    If left at room temperature, they become softer, juicier, and sweeter over several days.

  10. The world's largest peach producers are China, Spain, and Italy.

    China grows by far the most peaches, producing more than half of the world's total harvest.

Bonus fact: Peaches have a natural sweet fragrance that comes from dozens of aroma compounds. This distinctive smell becomes stronger as the fruit ripens, helping signal that the peach is ready to eat.
