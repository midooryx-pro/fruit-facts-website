Mangoes

  1. Mangoes are known as the "King of Fruits."

    In many parts of the world, especially South Asia, the mango is celebrated for its rich flavor, aroma, and cultural significance.

  2. They've been cultivated for over 4,000 years.

    Mangoes originated in South Asia and have been grown by humans for thousands of years, making them one of the oldest cultivated fruits.

  3. There are over 1,000 varieties of mangoes.

    They come in many shapes, sizes, and colors, including green, yellow, orange, red, and even purple.

  4. Mangoes are rich in vitamin C and vitamin A.

    A single ripe mango provides a large portion of your daily vitamin C needs and is also an excellent source of vitamin A for healthy vision and immunity.

  5. The largest mango producer is India.

    India grows more mangoes than any other country, producing around half of the world's supply.

  6. Mangoes belong to the same family as cashews and pistachios.

    They are members of the Anacardiaceae family, which also includes poison ivy and poison oak.

  7. The sap can irritate some people's skin.

    Mango peels and stems contain compounds related to those in poison ivy. People who are sensitive may experience skin irritation when handling fresh mangoes.

  8. Mangoes continue to ripen after they're picked.

    You can ripen a firm mango at room temperature, and placing it in a paper bag speeds up the process.

  9. The seed is surprisingly large.

    A mango's flat pit can take up 10–25% of the fruit's weight, depending on the variety.

  10. Mango trees can live for centuries.

    Healthy mango trees may continue producing fruit for 100 years or more, and some historic trees are believed to be over 300 years old.

Bonus fact: The sweet smell of a ripe mango comes from dozens of natural aroma compounds. Scientists have identified more than 300 different volatile compounds that contribute to the unique fragrance of different mango varieties.
